## The clashes

### 1. The impartiality smuggle (A1) — located to the word
- **Critic:** the load-bearing inference is invalid as written. The Null-Space Theorem forbids a privileged **epistemic** standpoint (total reach); the egoist's exemption claims a privileged **normative** standpoint (his satisfaction has authority). These are different totalities. **The equivocation is at line 464, in the word "*privileged*,"** which slides from *epistemically* privileged (forbidden by the theorem) to *normatively* privileged (untouched by it). The bridge premise — **impartiality** ("standing must be distributed evenly across apertures") — does the entire normative lift and is nowhere argued. It is Sidgwick/Nagel/Parfit's premise, *precisely what the egoist denies.*
- **Apologist (best defense):** the "co-" makes it **self-undermining, not merely inconsistent**: a *co*-constituted navigator's very agency is "drawn through the same relations it would disown," so to claim standing for his own navigating while denying it to others' "saws off the branch that holds up his own agency." This is *more* than universalizability — it's a claim about the metaphysics of the capacity being exercised, which the shmagent cannot decline the way he declines a maxim.
- **Verdict — SURVIVES (critic wins; three-engine corroboration).** The apologist's "saws off the branch" still needs the premise that *claiming standing requires honoring the relation that confers it* — which is the impartiality premise again. **Constitution-*by* R is not obligation-*to* R** (the child who audits and rejects his mother tongue was constituted *through* it and owes it nothing — the book's *own* example). The egoist grants the co-constitution *etiology* and denies the *obligation* — the is/ought gap the relational premise does nothing to close. A1 is now corroborated by **Fable + the metaphysics-critic (circled) + the metaethics-critic (located to l.464/"privileged")** and it defeats the apologist's strongest reply. *Fix: concede the floor rests partly on the wager (that navigators *count* is a bet); demote "the spine of this ethics and the refutation of its adversary are the same theorem"; the honest route to keeping the floor is to **argue impartiality directly, via Nagel** (uncited — see §8).*

### 2. The indexical egoist — does the theorem touch him at all?
- **Critic:** the genuinely dangerous figure is not the hypocrite (rule-for-others-exemption-for-self) but the **indexical egoist** who claims *only a location*: "I act from *here*; I make no claim that mine counts more in a view-from-nowhere ledger — I deny there is such a ledger." He affirms the theorem, universalizes nothing, is not incoherent. The book's ontology **supports him** — it removed the neutral standpoint from which "count equally" could even be *stated as a fact*. All three replies (performed-totality, exemption-refutation, consistency-fork) presuppose impartiality or universalizability, which he denies.
- **Apologist (best defense):** the fork closes — *exempt* (theorem forbids the privileged place) or *universalize* (licenses the world to override him → the dimensionless point). "There is no third position where you both retain the egoist's end and escape the norm."
- **Verdict — SURVIVES (critic wins; the deepest hole).** The apologist's "no third position" assumes the egoist's *end* is "privileged standing others lack." The indexical egoist's end is **"satisfy this index's preferences"** — pursued without any standing-claim, so he takes *neither* fork. The apologist's reply conflates pursuing-one's-own-index with claiming-privileged-standing. "The totality-standpoint performed rather than professed" is a **redescription, not an argument** — it relabels agent-relativity as disguised agent-neutrality, which is the very thesis in dispute since Nagel 1970. *Fix: stage the indexical/agent-relative egoist explicitly and either defeat him (requires a real argument that agent-relative weighting entails an agent-neutral totality-claim — hard) or concede the floor does not reach him and rests on the posited impartiality premise.*

### 3. Does "co-" answer Enoch, or restate Korsgaard?
- **Critic:** restates Korsgaard in relational vocabulary and hits Korsgaard's wall. Grant the metaphysics (agency relationally constituted) entirely — it *still* yields no ought: an is/ought gap. And the incoherence charge only bites "for one who values coherence," which the shmagent is *defined* not to. **Darwall's second-personal ethics is the uncited ancestor** (obligation from mutual address) and faces the identical Enochian objection.
- **Apologist (best defense):** the constituency claim is *modal, not formal* — "you cannot refuse to have been co-constituted the way you can refuse to adopt a maxim." That is the specific repair that closes the specific gap Enoch found; Ubuntu given a *constitutivist* (not merely communitarian) formulation, welded to the theorem.
- **Verdict — SURVIVES, but the apologist sharpens the real task.** The apologist wins the narrow point (the egoist can't deny the *fact* of co-constitution) but the critic wins the load-bearing one (that fact yields no *norm* — modal-constitution ≠ obligation). *Fix: both halves independently say **name Darwall and argue "constituency" as constitution-not-mere-formation** — the modal difference is what the anti-exemption move needs, and it is asserted more than argued in Part V. Pull one sentence of the Vygotsky/relational-ontology evidence into the spine to convert assertion → derivation. But do not claim it closes the is/ought gap; it doesn't.*

### 4. Moral facts as order-parameters — metaethics or a theorem's job done by a metaphor?
- **Critic:** relativism in a physics costume. **Damningly, it is unfalsifiable by the book's *own* standard**: the book insists (l.282) a claim differs from a definition by having failure regimes — and gives the Coherence Principle two — then the order-parameter ethics has *none* (any convention = "ordered phase," any dissent = "fluctuation," any atrocity-consensus = "a phase"). By l.282's own criterion it is **a definition, not a claim.** No symmetry group, no order-parameter field, no control parameter — the exact error the book prosecutes in IIT ("mistaking a coordinate for the country"), un-self-audited.
- **Apologist (best defense):** it does **metaethical (explanatory) work, not normative work** — it reconciles realism's datum (bindingness felt as law) with relativism's (variance), *both veridical* like a ferromagnet's magnetization (real state, objective-within-phase, contingent-across-phase); it beats quasi-realism because bindingness is *veridical-within-phase*, not projected. The normativity enters through the **separate** co-constitutive door, not through the magnet — so no is/ought fallacy. The Aquinas dimensionless-demon is a *cashed* prediction (a saturated order-parameter has no dynamics).
- **Verdict — CONTESTED → demote the physics, keep the reconciliation; and note the cross-domain convergence.** The apologist's "explanatory not normative" defuses the *ought-from-a-phase* charge cleanly — and the division of labor (order-parameter explains phenomenology/variance; floor binds) should be **stated explicitly** (it currently reads as one move). But it does **not** answer the critic's l.282 self-standard charge or the "no symmetry group" charge — and here the **physics panel is decisive**: the *physics-critic* independently calls it "decorative — none of the four Landau ingredients," and even the *physics-apologist* concedes it is "Landau-level, not microscopic" and its fix is to **name the symmetry as permutation-invariance over navigators.** *Fix: (a) state the explanatory/normative division of labor; (b) either name the symmetry (permutation-invariance — which, note, **is the impartiality premise of §1**, so naming it *exposes* A1 rather than hiding it) or soften "moral facts **are** order-parameters" → "**behave like**" (analogy, not identity), demoting the theorem-pretense per the book's own IIT critique.*

### 5. The developmental audit-clause (R3, added this session)
- **Critic:** a fresh circularity — it **smuggles a telos** (the future auditor) into an ethics that spent Parts I & V insisting there is no summit and no teleology ("a direction without a destination" — but the auditor-to-come *is* a destination). And "unauditable" **proves too much or too little**: if it means "practically hard to revise," it indicts most of childhood; if "logically impossible," almost nothing qualifies (apostates audit sealed indoctrinations). It survives only by hovering between the two.
- **Apologist (best defense):** the "crown jewel" — retrospective *auditability* replaces prospective *consent*, solving shaped-before-consent with *one* meta-capability, not a thick list. Against the "parochial autonomy-fetish" charge: it forbids *foreclosure*, doesn't mandate *exercise*; the capacity is **derived** (the Null-Space Theorem forbids self-transparency, so the demand is only that the exit-in-time not be sealed), so a deeply traditional upbringing runs *through* so long as the door stays open.
- **Verdict — CONTESTED → the clause is defensible but owes two repairs.** The apologist wins the parochialism fight decisively (foreclosure-not-exercise; derived-not-preferred). But the critic's **teleology** charge is a *different* objection the apologist doesn't meet — "oriented toward the future navigator's capacity to audit" *is* forward-looking, and the book disclaims teleology. And the **threshold** for "unauditable" is genuinely unspecified. *Fix (on my own R3 addition): (a) resolve the teleology tension — either concede the clause is mildly telic (and soften "no destination") or find a non-forward-looking criterion; (b) supply the auditable/unauditable threshold.*

### 6. Diffuse structural over-running (R8 Move 2, added this session)
- **Critic:** a **Trojan horse for distributive justice.** Everywhere else the floor is thin and refuses distributive content ("will not adjudicate your tax policy"); but "whole classes of navigator with structurally less room to move" *requires* a baseline of how-much-room-is-owed and a metric of navigable range — the substantive theory the floor disclaims. Either the floor stays thin and *cannot* reach structural injustice (the paragraph is wishful), or it reaches it and is *not thin*.
- **Apologist (best defense):** it's structural violence (Galtung, Farmer) + epistemic injustice (Fricker) given a first-principles home; the floor is thin along the axis of *the good* and load-bearing along the axis of *coercion, erasure, and the distribution of freedom* — the right division. The inequality-of-null-spaces result (the powerful can afford blindness) gives it teeth.
- **Verdict — CONTESTED → keep the clause, concede it owes a metric.** The apologist wins the *principle* (freedom-vs-the-good is a defensible line). The critic wins the *cash*: "structurally less room to move" needs a metric of navigable-range the book doesn't supply. *Fix: keep the diffuse-coercion clause but either sketch the (thin) navigable-range metric or downgrade to "a violation the framework can name but not yet measure" — the critic's own honest alternative.*

### 7. The two-evils asymmetry still empty (R2, added this session)
- **Critic:** even granting the theorem refutes "I am the totality," it reaches **only a cartoon Satan** who literally professes totality (which "almost no actual predator" does — the book concedes it), and the "performed totality" repair is the §2 redescription. Worse, "demonic = dimensionless" **mislocates the finished atrocity as morally-empty** rather than maximally-evil — but our intuition says the sealed torture-state *is* the worst thing, not "off the axis."
- **Apologist (best defense) + the physics panel:** the dimensionless-demon is a *cashed* order-parameter prediction — and **both physics reviewers confirm the formal move is correct** ("a saturated order parameter has no local variance/dynamics"). It matches the theological tradition's reluctant conclusion (the damned are *diminished*, not superlative).
- **Verdict — CONTESTED → the physics is sound, the ethics is costly; address the intuition head-on.** The formal claim (locked pole = dimensionless) is *right* (physics-critic + physics-apologist both grant it). But the metaethics-critic's normative point **survives**: an account on which the completed genocide is "morally dimensionless" rather than "maximally evil" has to *earn* that against a strong contrary intuition. *Fix (on my R2 addition): meet "isn't the finished atrocity maximally evil?" directly — defend why "off-axis" is not a mislocation (the evil was in the *lived, still-open* descent; the corpse-state is its terminus, not its maximum), or concede this as a real cost of the geometry.*

## The one thing the apologist wins outright
**"Validity, not grip" is the *correct sizing* of the amoralist problem, not a confession of defeat** — *no* theory converts the willing shrugger by proof (realist, divine-command, Kantian, contractualist all shrug-vulnerable); the book's advance is to *build* grip through open exits, community, and attention rather than pretend to prove it. This is worth **adding to the text** as a claimed advantage (two sentences), per the apologist's constructive note. It does **not**, however, rescue A1–A3: the critic's point is that even the *validity* the book claims goes undemonstrated against the indexical egoist — which is a separate, and winning, charge.
