## What it is
Traces one persistent narrative structure across Indo-European folktale, myth, scripture, modern lit: **can humans obtain transformative power without paying its cost?** and how cultures manage the debt of progress — by *evasion* (folktale), *embodiment* (Prometheus), *transfer/spiritualization* (Christ), or *contamination* (modern lit).
- **ATU 330 "The Smith and the Devil"** (Aarne–Thompson–Uther): smith + supernatural adversary + bargain + extraordinary power + attempt to escape the pact. da Silva & Tehrani 2016 phylogenetics: strongest support at **Proto-Indo-European** level (posterior 0.87) — ~6000-yr deep-time narrative. The **smith = threshold figure into technical culture** (transforms matter through fire/skill). "Human technical power may carry a debt."
- **Scapegoat logic** (Girard, uncited but operative): "the accumulated disorder of human relation is concentrated onto one exposed body"; the community gets relief through the body that bears its violence "while forgetting to ask why such a body had to be produced."
- **Dispersed → concentrated cost:** landscape (Gilgamesh's cedar forest) → blood/kinship (Cain) → institutional language (Babel) → the exposed body (Christ). The tree as civilization's recurring symbol.
- **Modern skepticism** (Mann *Doctor Faustus*; Madách *Tragedy of Man*): no evasion, no theological consolation — "every ideal is false… ideals become compromised when they enter history"; "every attempt to build that desire into a world **produces a remainder**." Conclusion: modernity removes the bargain's *simplicity*; the question becomes whether we can "recognize the cost of our own aspirations without either denying the cost or giving up aspiration."
