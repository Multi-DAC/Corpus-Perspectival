## The core claims (intellectual payload, stripped of the biography + ad reads)

1. **Consciousness is fundamental; boot physics FROM consciousness.** Hoffman's *conscious realism*: reality consists of and only of conscious agents interacting; physical reality is *derived*. Gallimore states the flip in our exact words: *"rather than this failed program of assuming that matter is fundamental and trying to boot up consciousness from dead inert physical matter."*
2. **The interface / icon.** We never perceive the conscious-agent network directly (effectively infinite); what we perceive is an **interface** of **icons** that lets us act adaptively. *"Everything's an icon." "Fitness before truth."* (Hoffman's FBT theorem: evolution tunes perception to fitness, not to veridical structure.)
3. **Experience space + the qualia kernel (a Markov kernel).** The *experience space* = the set of all possible states a conscious agent can occupy. On top sits a **Markov kernel** ("qualia kernel") giving it **dynamics** — transition probabilities between experience-states. You normally sit in one narrow region. **★ The qualia kernel is composed of THREE kernels: perception, decision, and action.**
4. **The title thesis — "DMT didn't take you somewhere new."** DMT does not transport you to another *place*. It changes the **qualia kernel / the region** of experience-space you occupy — *"it's like the headset has been changed," "a different interface," "a different region of the experience base."* The other regions (and the conscious agents in them) were always there; the lens re-aimed. The "entities" = conscious agents in a region you normally can't perceive. (Gallimore's strong extension: they're *real, sentient* — graded speculative below.)
5. **Collapse into new order.** DMT phenomenology = brief spike of randomness → the brain *"suddenly collapses into this new order"* — old order (waking world-model) → fluid → collapse to a novel coherent regime at "breakthrough."
6. **One unified consciousness; all experiences equally real.** *"Ultimately there is one unified consciousness… Brahman… all experiences have the same ontological validity, all as real as each other."* He keeps the word *illusion* but immediately walks it back ("doesn't mean it's not real").

## ⚠ The load-bearing DIVERGENCE (do not paper over)

**Hoffman's "fitness before truth / everything's an icon" is in real tension with the book's woo-guard.** Hoffman: the interface is a *useful fiction* — the icon does **not** resemble the true (agent-network) reality; perception hides reality to serve fitness (desktop-icon metaphor). *The Inside View*: the projection is **REAL** — experience is the only realness; there is **no truer hidden reality the experience is a lie about.** These are **not** the same claim. Hoffman keeps a representational gap (icon ≠ reality); the framework **collapses** it (the experienced region *is* a real region of the one substrate, not a fiction over it).

Tellingly, **Gallimore himself wobbles exactly here** — "this reality is an illusion… but doesn't mean it's not real… all as real as each other." The framework's woo-guard ("drop *illusion/hologram/simulation* entirely; the projection is real") is the **cleaner settling of his own ambivalence.** This divergence is a *feature* for us: it sharpens the woo-guard by contrast, and it's the line I'd hold if anyone tried to collapse our view into Hoffman's.
