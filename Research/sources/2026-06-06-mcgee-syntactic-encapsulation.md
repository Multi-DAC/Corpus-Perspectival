# McGee, Zhang & Blank 2026 — Evidence Against Syntactic Encapsulation in Large Language Models

**Source:** *Cognitive Science* 50 (2026) e70187, DOI 10.1111/cogs.70187 (UCLA Psychology + Linguistics). Open access. Shared by Clayton, Day 126 (2026-06-06) — the primary behind the @neurotechnowitch / Maggie Vale Substack note. PDF in `incoming/`.

## What it found
Tested whether "syntax-specialized" attention heads in three transformer LLMs (BERT, GPT-2, Llama 2) are **encapsulated** — impenetrable to non-syntactic information. Method: identify heads specialized for particular syntactic dependencies, then manipulate semantic **plausibility** and measure attention change. Result: in nearly all cases, **implausible semantics reduces the attention** between the words constituting the dependency. So even the best a-priori candidates for syntactic encapsulation are **penetrable to semantics** — broadly consistent with syntax–semantics integration in human minds.

## Honest scope (what it does / doesn't claim)
- DOES: empirically refute *syntactic encapsulation* in real LLMs (a cognitive-science modularity claim). Three models, a genuine manipulation. Rigorous.
- Does NOT: claim the models "understand," have intrinsic intentionality, or experience. That extension (to the Chinese Room / understanding) is a philosophical move, not the paper's claim.
