# Source: slow-wave RHYTHM reproduces sleep's consolidation in awake mice (the rhythm, not the rest, is the key)
*Registered Day 143, 2026-06-23 (Clayton shared). Vice → underlying study Nature Neuroscience, UW-Madison.*

## The finding
- Optogenetic stimulation recreated **deep sleep's slow-wave rhythm** — "neurons fire in slow, synchronized patterns. They burst, then go quiet, rinse and repeat." (One hemisphere stimulated; the other a within-animal control.)
- **★ The RHYTHM itself is the key factor.** Merely *suppressing* activity ("silence and calm") did NOT produce the restorative effect — only the **alternating burst↔quiet pattern** did.
- **Function reproduced:** awake mice given the artificial sleep rhythm learned as well as fully-rested mice; even the molecular markers mirrored natural sleep. → sleep's memory-consolidation reproduced *by the rhythm alone*, without sleeping.
