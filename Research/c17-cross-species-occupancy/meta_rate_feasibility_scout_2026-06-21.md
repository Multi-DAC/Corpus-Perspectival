# Cross-species meta-rate — feasibility scout (Day 141, 2026-06-21, w/ Clayton)

**Question:** does paired cross-species data exist to test the re-bet — that a *measurable* meta-binding rate clusters/decouples from CFFT across species (A152, the LC53 graduation gate)? CFFT was the wrong λ; is the right λ measurable cross-species?

## Verdict: YES — but NOT via the measure we planned. The winning axis is CORTICAL OSCILLATION FREQUENCY, and the test is largely ALREADY RUN.

### What's data-starved (as predicted)
- **Multisensory temporal binding window (TBW) across species** — the measure I'd planned to lead with — is **human-dominant**. Direct cross-species behavioral data is thin: essentially **human + rat** matched (TOJ), plus monkey/cat by *heterogeneous* paradigms. The structural problem stands: the **extreme-CFFT species (insects ~300 Hz, fast birds)** that would make the contrast sharp have **no binding-window measure**, and the species *with* binding windows (human/rat/monkey/cat) sit in a **narrow CFFT band** (~all vertebrate, tens of Hz). So a clean TBW-vs-CFFT decoupling test at n≥5 is **not runnable on existing TBW data**. The scout did its job: that path is data-limited.

### ★★ The pivot — the meta-rate IS measured cross-species, as brain-rhythm frequency
**Buzsáki, Logothetis & Singer (2013), "Scaling Brain Size, Keeping Timing: Evolutionary Preservation of Brain Rhythms," *Neuron* (PMC4009705 / PubMed 24183025).** The headline IS our prediction:
- Across a **17,000-fold** range of mammalian brain volume, **alpha/beta/gamma oscillation frequencies are roughly CONSTANT** — "multiple-time-scale communication... at approximately the same speed, irrespective of brain size."
- **Conduction velocity varies widely** (the substrate/"peripheral"-analog that's free to diverge) — preserved timing is bought by compensating architecture (large-diameter long-range axons). I.e. *the system pays to keep the binding rate fixed while the substrate scales.*
- Residual variation exists (theta 6–10 Hz rodents → 4–6 Hz carnivores → 1–4 Hz humans) — so it's a **compressed band, not a rigid clock** (same honest grade as the within-human 13× result).

**This is the cross-species level-separation, sourced and POSITIVE:** the binding/integration rate (cortical rhythms) is ~conserved exactly where the physical substrate (brain size 17,000×, conduction velocity) varies wildly — the cross-species twin of the within-human "binding window 13× more invariant than peripheral acuity." And it matches the framework's *positive* prediction: the meta-rate tracks the **global-integration architecture** (kept fixed on purpose — Buzsáki's "temporal organizational priority"), NOT the sensor speed.

### Behavioral corroboration (weak, n=2 but matched)
**Tomassini et al., "Temporal order judgment of multisensory stimuli in rat and human," *Front. Behav. Neurosci.* 2022 (PMC9879721).** Matched audiovisual TOJ: rats and humans both operate in the **tens-to-hundreds-of-ms** binding regime (rats better <100 ms SOA, humans better >100 ms) — i.e. their binding precision is **same-order**, NOT separated by any large sensor ratio. Consistent with a conserved binding scale.

### Peripheral axis (the contrast term) — rich
**Healy et al. 2013, "Metabolic rate and body size are linked with perception of temporal information"** — CFFT phylogenetic comparative dataset, many vertebrates, ~75× span. This is the "free-to-diverge sensor" axis, already compiled.

## So the study IS runnable — reframed
The clean version is **not** "TBW vs CFFT across n≥5" (data-starved). It's:
1. **Meta-rate axis = cortical oscillation frequency** (Buzsáki et al.): ~invariant across 17,000× brain size.
2. **Substrate/peripheral axis = brain size + conduction velocity (Buzsáki) and CFFT (Healy):** vary by 10³–10⁴× and ~75×.
3. **Result already in the literature:** decoupling/preservation = POSITIVE. The meta-rate conserves where the substrate diverges.
4. **Our contribution:** frame it as the C17/LC53/LC55 prediction confirmed at the *proper level* (the original CFFT falsification was off-level), quantify the compression (substrate span ÷ rhythm span), and add the behavioral (rat-human TOJ) + within-human (13×) tiers → a **three-tier convergence** (within-system rod↔cone / within-human cross-modal / cross-species brain-rhythm) all showing the same shape.

## Honest grade (resist CONFIRMATION_SEEKING — I wanted this rescued)
- **STRONG:** the cross-species conservation is real, sourced (Buzsáki = a major review), and at the right level.
- **Caveats:** (a) brain rhythms are the **neural correlate** of the binding rate, not the phenomenal "now" itself — a bridging assumption; (b) **mammal-centric** — insects/birds (extreme CFFT) aren't bridged, so the *sharpest* contrast (300 Hz CFFT vs binding rate) is still indirect; (c) **band, not clock** (theta still varies ~3–4× across mammalian orders). Defensible claim: *"the meta-binding rate is conserved across species at a level far more invariant than CFFT or brain size — graduating LC53 from conjecture to sourced cross-species support — but it is a compressed band measured neurally, not a single phenomenal clock."*
