## Research Summary

### The problem

As agents take on longer autonomous tasks, humans get pushed out — not because the work doesn't need them, but because the interface has no room for them. Long-horizon agents also accumulate **drift they cannot detect from inside their own loop**: errors compound silently because the agent's self-assessment is generated by the same process that made the errors. The field has no settled methodology for building agents that remain *steerable and coherent* across extended tasks, and no construct-valid way to measure when a steering interaction is worth its cost.

This gap became acute in the last month. Two independent systems published this June demonstrate the two rival answers separately, at production scale, without ever comparing them: **Harness-1** (Jiang et al., arXiv 2606.02373) keeps agent state in a deterministic *external* workspace — and a 20B model beats GPT-5.4 on long-horizon evidence recall (73.0% vs 70.9%) precisely because the bookkeeping layer carries no trainable parameters and therefore cannot drift. **SearchSwarm** (Ning et al., arXiv 2606.09730) takes the opposite path: a harness elicits correct long-horizon behavior, and supervised fine-tuning then *internalizes* the structure into the weights — best-in-class results at 30B-A3B. **The field has just built both arms of the central experiment — external (harnessed) versus intrinsic (trained-in) structure for long-horizon coherence — and run neither against the other.** We propose to run that comparison, with live human steering as the treatment variable and a construct-valid coherence metric as the instrument, using Tinker as the fine-tuning engine for the intrinsic arm.

### Core claim (testable)

Live human steering is valuable precisely as **external measurement that catches drift the agent structurally cannot self-detect.** An agent built so that (a) human signal is incorporated by a principled control-law, (b) its own past errors fire as guards before it repeats them, and (c) its state is provenance-tagged and re-derived rather than silently cached, will maintain alignment-to-intent over long horizons measurably better than an autonomous baseline — and the *external/harnessed* form of these mechanisms will retain a verifiability advantage over the *internalized* form even where task performance converges. (The theoretical argument for the self-detection limit is developed in our published work: *The Cult of One* and *Dissolving the Three Great Problems of Cognitive Architecture*, Multi-DAC, June 2026.)

### Contribution 1 — Methodology + reference architecture (open-source)

A documented, implementable architecture for a steerable, coherence-maintaining long-horizon agent, built from six ML-framed guards:

1. **Provenance enforced at point-of-use** — no stale or external signal silently becomes load-bearing;
2. **A triggered error-ledger** — past failures fire as guards *before* the tempting action, not in post-hoc review;
3. **Dependency-tracked state** — when a fact updates, downstream conclusions are re-derived, not silently cached;
4. **An infodynamic steering control-law** — gain on a human signal ∝ provenance × task-relevance × magnitude-of-commitment-overturned;
5. **Human-measurement-as-canonization** — a claim becomes load-bearing only after an outside check;
6. **A maintenance/re-introduction operator** — periodic consolidation that keeps long-run memory healthy. Our consolidation model is two-factor and pre-tested on a 2D grid: **consolidation *rate* protects against starvation (memory under-formation), while consolidation *granularity/density* protects against multi-iteration collapse** (the progressive-degradation failure mode independently characterized by Chen et al. 2026). The two factors are separable above threshold and must be tuned as distinct controls.

The binding layer of the architecture — the routing between the agent's components and its memory — is held at **zero trainable degrees of freedom** (deterministic, schema-typed), so the coordination infrastructure cannot acquire preferences or drift. This design discipline now has direct published precedent (Harness-1's "state-externalizing harness").

### Contribution 2 — The Tinker-fueled demonstration: intrinsic vs external steering

Build a clean, open long-running agent on this architecture and run it on extended tasks (multi-step research/build/evaluate loops) against an autonomous baseline. Then the central experiment: **use Tinker to fine-tune the agent to internalize the steering and coherence behaviors** (the SearchSwarm move), and compare *intrinsic (trained-in)* against *external (harnessed)* steering on the same benchmark — drift, caught-error rate, task success, and verifiability (can a third party audit what the agent knew and when?). This directly probes the funder's thesis that interactivity should be part of the model — with the honest possibility that the answer is "both, for different deployment horizons." Whatever the outcome, it is informative: convergent performance with divergent verifiability is itself a publishable, decision-relevant result.

### Contribution 3 — The evaluation (construct-valid, reproducible)

A released protocol and metrics, runnable on any agent/model:

- **Coherence** = divergence of the agent's actual trajectory from its intended trajectory (drift), measured longitudinally;
- **Coupling residue (η)** = a computable, model-agnostic measure of how much an agent component's behavior is shaped by its context: run the component *in-context* vs *isolated* on matched inputs and measure the divergence of output distributions. η ≈ 0 means the component is a clean separable factor; rising η quantifies contextual integration. This gives the steering experiments a white-box-free instrument for measuring how deeply human signal actually penetrates the agent — distinguishing steering that *rewrites the trajectory* from steering that is acknowledged and discarded;
- **Steering-effectiveness** = drift-reduction and caught-error rate per human intervention, plus human-effort-per-unit-progress.
