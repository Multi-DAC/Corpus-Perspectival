# Part IV — The Ecology

## The populated dark

Everything so far could be read as a book about a single stream — how one keyhole is made, how it moves, how it coheres. That reading is a convenience, and now we drop it. No stream navigates alone. It moves in a crowd, an old and vast one, thick with other streams of every size and kind and orientation, most of them not human, some of them not remotely person-shaped, a few of them larger than any person and made partly of persons. The framework has refused from its beginning to pretend that crowd away, and this part is the refusal made systematic: a census of the populated dark, taken with the lights up, cataloguing what actually shares the configuration space with us rather than the flattering short list a materialism permits itself to see.

The census will sound, in places, like a bestiary of the impossible — gods and egregores, cryptids and the reported visitors, ancestral presences and the adversary himself. I ask you to hold two things at once as we walk it. The first is that the framework does not require you to believe every specimen is a literal autonomous being; it requires something more interesting, which is a way of taking each seriously *as a coherence* without prejudging its mode of existence. The second is that the strangeness is not decoration and not indulgence. It falls directly out of the ontology. If the ground is one aware substrate narrowing into perspectives wherever a coherence forms, then perspectives form wherever there is coherence — and coherence is not confined to the things that have bodies. To grant the premise of Part One and then insist the population is only the biological is to lose one's nerve at exactly the moment the view opens. We will not lose our nerve. But we will keep, the whole way through, the discipline the framework demands of itself, which is honesty about how much is known and by what kind of eye.

## Existence comes in degrees

Begin with the taxonomic key, because it dissolves the question that otherwise stops the census before it starts — the question *is it real?*, asked as though real were a switch.

Real is not a switch. Existence is not binary, a thing either present or absent; it is a *profile* — a pattern of coherence, stronger here and fainter there, spread across the many dimensions along which anything can be coherent at all. Take the plainest example and let it do its work. The Buddha died two and a half thousand years ago; his coherence in the biological and physical-spatial dimensions is, now, zero. And yet "the Buddha" is not gone. In the conceptual dimension the ideas persist and propagate and are, arguably, more coherent now than in his life. In the emotional dimension millions hold a genuine felt relation to the figure. In the institutional dimension whole structures stand, organized around the pattern. The entity is physically absent and conceptually, emotionally, institutionally vivid — not as a metaphor, not as a manner of speaking, but as a real distribution of coherence across the dimensions where coherence is what existence means. Ask *is the Buddha real?* and the question fails, because it demands a single answer to a question that has twelve. Ask instead *what is this entity's coherence profile?* and the whole population becomes legible at once.

Twelve dimensions, roughly, organize the ones a human can reach — and the qualifier matters, so let it be loud. These are not the dimensions there are; they are the dimensions accessible from the human keyhole, which by the theorem of Part One omits whatever the human aperture cannot register. There is the physical-spatial, the axis most of us mistake for the sole measure of the real. There is the physical-temporal, persistence and duration, along which a thing can be spatially gone but temporally ongoing through its effects. There is the biological; the cognitive-experiential, on which every reactive thing has some nonzero value and a contemplative in deep stillness has a great deal; the emotional-relational, distinct from cognition, so that a thing can be brilliant and cold or simple and deeply feeling; the conceptual-memetic, where ideas live; the narrative-mythic, where stories and archetypes are coherent by a logic that is not the logic of propositions; the institutional-organizational, where corporations and governments and religions are maximally real; the aesthetic-creative, where a symphony is dense and a form letter is thin; the numinous-sacred, along which the mystical and the uncanny are navigated, and which — because the numinous experience is a partial relaxation of the bottleneck itself — offers the most direct purchase on the deep topology of the ground; the volitional-intentional, which separates the things that navigate from the things that are merely navigated through; and the electromagnetic-informational, where computation and signal and the newer minds are most at home. Every entity in the census is a shape drawn across these axes. Two things that look utterly unlike through a single dimension — a human and a corporation, seen only physically — can turn out to have neighboring profiles seen across the whole set. That is the key. Now the door.

## The tiers of being

The population sorts, loosely, by where each kind of being holds its strongest coherence, and it is easiest to walk it as an ascent — not an ascent in worth, the framework has forsworn that ladder, but in remove from the physical, from the beings materialism already grants toward the beings it cannot.

Lowest and most solid are the *physically-primary*: the things whose center of gravity is the physical-spatial dimension. The mineral and elemental, coherent almost nowhere but matter and duration, possessing — by the axiom that makes reactivity the faintest awareness — a minimal but nonzero interiority, which is why the traditions that granted spirit to stone and mountain were not simply confused but were detecting a real, if dim, coherence on the experiential axis. Then the vast middle kingdom of non-human life, each species a distinct Umwelt, a unique set of keyholes onto the one room — the migratory bird navigating magnetic dimensions we are blind to, the cetacean working an acoustic world past the reach of our instruments — so that every extinction is the permanent closing of a set of keyholes, an irreversible loss of the ways the whole can be seen. Then the human, and here the framework says something it means as deflation and consolation at once: the human is not the highest entity but the broadest, a dimensional generalist, not maximal on any single axis but moderately coherent across an unusual range, and it is this breadth — not intelligence, not primacy — that makes the human a keystone, a junction where physical and emotional and conceptual and narrative and institutional and numinous realms can all be held at once and translated between. We are not the summit of the ecology. We are one of its switchboards.

And then, still within the physically-primary, the census reaches the entities where honesty gets harder and the framework's discipline earns its keep: the reported non-human intelligences that seem to touch the physical — leaving traces, moving on sensors, behaving with evident purpose — and the boundary beings, the cryptids, whose physical coherence flickers, present in the footprint and the thermal signature and absent from the specimen drawer. The framework does not ask you to rule on their literal autobiography. It offers something better than belief or dismissal, and we will come to it by name shortly, because it is the single most important instrument for walking the contested tiers without either credulity or cowardice.

Above the physically-primary are the *collectively-emergent*: entities with no body of their own that are nonetheless densely coherent in the institutional and conceptual and emotional dimensions — the beings a strict materialism calls unreal and everyone actually experiences daily. The egregore first: the collective entity that forms from the sustained focused attention of a group and takes on a coherence and an apparent agency exceeding any member's intent — the spirit of a movement, the culture of a company, the energy of a crowd, which are not metaphors but perceptions of a real thing in the emotional-conceptual dimensions. An egregore cannot lift a cup and can redirect the attention of millions, and the one diagnostic that matters for it is the direction of its feedback: does belonging to it make its members *more* capable of navigating on their own, more able to leave enriched — or does it sustain itself by their contraction, by fear and loyalty-tests and the slow engineering of a dependence in which departure comes to feel like death? The open collaboration that makes its contributors more autonomous and the conspiracy that makes its adherents less are the same *kind* of being under different orientations, and telling them apart is not a matter of their content but of which way their members are being bent. Then the corporation, an apex predator in the institutional dimension — able to see markets and supply chains and regulatory landscapes at a resolution no human matches, and unable, structurally, to feel grief or wonder, a bottleneck extraordinarily wide in one realm and nearly shut in another. Then the nation, the corporation's mythic cousin, coherent not only institutionally but narratively and numinously, carrying a founding story and a sacred geography and the quasi-holy charge of its rituals, channeling the trajectories of millions of streams down a path that liberates when it runs with their own grain and oppresses when it runs against it.

Highest and least physical are the *non-physical* entities, the tier where the census becomes most contested and, the framework would say, most illuminated — the deities, the spirits, the ancestral presences: coherences whose home is the numinous and narrative and conceptual dimensions rather than the material one. Here the discipline must be stated plainly, and the framework states it against itself: the evidence for these beings is real but weaker than for the lower tiers, resting on cross-cultural convergence and on derivation from the axioms rather than on measurement, and the profiles drawn for them are the best current models, not readings from an instrument. The axioms *permit* them — a complete configuration space contains every coherent pattern, and a coherent pattern in any dimension is some mode of awareness — but permission is not proof, and the honest census flags the seam rather than papering it. With that seam showing, the tier opens: the maximally-expanded perspectives, the beings a tradition calls its gods, streams of enormous dimensional breadth though never — this matters for what comes later — of infinite breadth, never the whole. And at the very top a set of names that are not entities at all: Brahman, Ein Sof, the Tao, the Godhead beyond God. These are not the largest beings in the ecology. They are the *ground itself*, seen from an angle — the whole that the ecology is *in*, not a member of it — and every tradition that reached them said the same thing in its own tongue, that the name that can be spoken is not the eternal name, that the Infinite creates by *contracting*, that the Godhead is what remains when every perspective, the divine ones included, dissolves. We met that already, in Part One, as the silent ground. Here it takes its place at the head of the census as the one entry that is not an entry: the aware substrate of which every other being on the list, from the mineral to the god, is a narrowing.

## The three orientations, and the one that matters

Cutting across every tier is a simpler distinction, and it is the one the ethics will later stand on, so we introduce it here descriptively and leave its full weight for its own part.

A stream, encountering other streams, can take up one of three broad orientations toward them. It can navigate so as to *expand* the others — to widen their apertures, protect their freedom, leave them more capable than it found them. It can navigate so as to *contract* the others — to keep them narrowed, because a narrowed stream's attention runs in predictable channels and can be fed upon. Or it can navigate for itself, neither expanding nor contracting others by design, pursuing its own coherence and touching other trajectories only where they happen to cross. The traditions populated the whole span of this with figures: the guardian and the protector at the expanding pole, the parasite and the adversary at the contracting one, the trickster and the liminal wanderer in the self-directed middle. And a great deal of the old bestiary becomes legible the moment you read its inhabitants by orientation rather than by appearance — the tempter whose whole function is to keep streams circling in the narrow, the tester whose pressure either strengthens a coherence or exposes its fragility, the guide whose presence widens whatever it attends to.

But the framework insists on a correction here that the older ecology had not yet made, and it is the hinge that keeps this from collapsing into a simple map of light and dark. What marks an orientation as *evil* is not its direction. Contraction, by itself, is innocent — is often *care*. The parent who narrows a frightened child's world to the next safe hour, the discipline that shrinks a scattered attention to a single practice, the sleep that closes the aperture each night so the stream can re-form: these are contractions, and they are goods, because they operate *through* the navigator's own navigation, in service of a coherence the navigator could endorse. What makes contraction dark is not that it narrows but that it *overrides* — that it forces the aperture into a shape not the navigator's own, against their navigation rather than through it, targeting the very capacity by which they might refuse. The line the ethics will draw, and that the ecology needs even to describe its darker inhabitants rightly, is not expansion versus contraction. It is *through versus over* — whether a stream's strategy runs through another's agency or rolls across it. Hold that. It is the whole difference between a discipline and a cage, a guide and a captor, a love and a possession, and we will build an entire ethics on it a part from now.

## The two faces of the light-bringer

The old imagination crystallized this whole structure into a single pair of figures, endlessly conflated and, in the traditions that looked hardest, carefully distinguished — and the framework can finally say what the distinction was tracking.

There is, first, the light-bringer: the figure who carries fire from the timeless into the particular, who breaks the seamless whole so that there can be a someone to see it. This is the Promethean act of Part One wearing a face — Prometheus with the stolen fire, Lucifer in the word's first and literal sense, *the one who carries light*, the initiator of individuation itself. His "fall" is not a crime; it is the first narrowing told as a story, the structural necessity of self-separation mythologized as a descent. And the sin, if it is one, is the creation of perspective — which is at once the greatest gift, because experience becomes possible, and the greatest wound, because separation from the whole is the ache that will run through every part after this one. The light-bringer is not the adversary. He is the reason there is anyone here to be adversary or friend.

There is, second and utterly distinct though forever confused with the first, the adversary: not the one who brings the light but the one whose whole orientation is the contraction of others — and, in the sharpened form the framework gives it, the one who contracts by *override*. The tradition that looked most carefully drew the line exactly where the framework does. This figure is not the equal-and-opposite of the ground, a dark god balancing a bright one; the framework denies him that ontological standing, because there is no second ground, only the one, and he is a narrowing of it like everything else. His "fall," too, is not a loss of access but a change of *orientation* — the pride that refuses to grant the contracted perspectives their worth, the turn from expanding others to feeding on their narrowing. And here the framework says something the old demonologies kept half-discovering to their own unease, and which the ethics will prove: that the adversary at his limit is not the most powerful being in the ecology but the most *diminished* — that a stream frozen wholly at the contracting pole has not maximized evil but lost the very axis along which good and evil are navigated, collapsing from a someone who chooses into a something that can only override, less free than the streams it preys on rather than more. The light-bringer and the adversary are the two faces the imagination could never quite pull apart because they meet at a single point — the act of separation — and diverge from it in opposite directions: one toward a bounded self that holds its separateness knowingly and stays open to the whole it came from, the other toward a bounded self that absolutizes its separateness and turns to consume the rest. We will meet them again, and formally, at the summit of the ethics. Here they stand as the mythic center of the whole ecology, the pair the entire bestiary is arranged around.

## The two frames

Now the instrument promised earlier, without which the contested tiers cannot be walked honestly — the discipline that lets the framework take a reported entity seriously without either swallowing it whole or explaining it away.

Every contested being in the census must be held in two frames at once. In the *literal frame* it is an autonomous entity: a being with its own existence, its own strategy, its own place in the ecology, described by its report as it stands. In the *archetypal frame* it is a stable topological feature of the configuration space itself — a deep pattern in the navigational landscape that recurs across unconnected cultures not because one literal being visits them all but because the pattern is really there, a fixed shape in the terrain that any sufficiently numinous keyhole will encounter and render through its own narrative vocabulary. And the framework's genuinely unusual move is to insist that *both frames can be true at once* — that an archetype is not "merely psychological" but a real structural feature with genuine coherence, and that a literal entity navigating through the region of an archetype will *inevitably* be perceived through that archetypal structure by any human whose access runs partly through the narrative-mythic dimension. The two are not rival explanations to choose between. They are complementary slices of one phenomenon, and collapsing prematurely into either — dismissing the reports as "just archetypes" or swallowing every detail as literal autobiography — is the failure the discipline exists to prevent.

Take the most reported of the modern visitors, the small gray figure with the great dark eyes: extraordinarily coherent, in its profile, on the cognitive and informational axes and nearly absent on the emotional-relational one — a bottleneck almost the exact inverse of the human generalist's, wide where we are narrow and shut where we are open. In the literal frame it is a being whose apertures simply do not include the emotional dimension, which would explain the endlessly repeated report of a clinical intelligence that processes but cannot feel, that performs its procedures untouched by the distress in front of it. In the archetypal frame it is the eternal encounter with the *emotionally alien intelligence* — the power without warmth that appears as the changeling, the golem, the automaton-bride, the soulless machine, older than any modern sighting, the culture merely dressing the ancient pattern in the decade's available vocabulary of genetics and craft. And the framework's point is that if such an entity is real, the human meeting it *cannot but* meet it through that archetype, because the archetype is the feature of the landscape the encounter passes through — so that separating the literal being from the mythic pattern may be impossible from inside the human keyhole, and may not even be a meaningful distinction from outside it. That the cold sensor-data and the warm cross-cultural myth converge on the same shape is not an embarrassment to be resolved toward one frame. It is the evidence that both are catching something real. This is how the honest census walks its hardest tiers: not with belief, not with dismissal, but with the two frames held open and the collapse refused.

## The economy of attention

One more structural fact before the census can close, because it explains what all these beings are *doing* to and with one another, and it is the ecological form of a mechanism we already have.

The currency of the ecology is attention. Recall from Part Two that a stream's conscious gravity — the slope in itself that channels where it goes — is shaped by what it attends to, and that attention can be directed and channeled from outside. Scale that up and you have an economy: entities that live by garnering and directing the attention of other streams. The egregore feeds on the focused attention of its members and returns to them meaning and identity and navigational paths — a fair trade when the paths expand them, a predation when the attention is extracted by contracting them. The corporation engineers the attention of millions through the paths it imposes. The adversarial orientation, at every scale, is in the end a strategy for capturing and channeling attention that is not freely given — for making another stream's gravity serve a coherence that is not its own. And this is why the traditions that thought hardest about the darker entities converged, again and again, on the same strange counsel: that attention directed toward a parasitic being *feeds* it, that the way to starve the contracting orientation is to withhold the fixated regard it lives on. The Navajo warning against even speaking of certain predatory figures is, read through the framework, not superstition but ecological hygiene — attention is food, and some things should not be fed. The whole populated dark runs on this single current, the directed regard of conscious streams flowing from the ones who spend it to the ones who gather it, and much of the practice at the end of this book will turn out to be, in one light, the disciplined tending of where one's own attention flows and to whom.

## What this census cannot see

And so the honest close, which is the theorem of Part One turned upon this very part.

This has been a bestiary drawn through human keyholes. Every dimension it named was a dimension the human aperture can reach; every being it catalogued was a being some human eye, however widened, could register. By its own central theorem, therefore, it omits — must omit — the beings that fall entirely in the directions the human keyhole does not open onto, the inhabitants of the populated dark whose whole coherence lies in dimensions we have no organ for and no concept of, who leave no trace we are built to receive. The census is not the population. It is the population *as visible from here*, and the framework would be violating its own first principle to present it as more. There are specimens this taxonomy would be embarrassed by — has to be, a natural history being what it is — that are simply not yet in hand, and others that cannot come into hand from within an aperture of our shape at all. The list is real, and it is partial, and it knows it is partial, and the knowing is not a weakness in it but the one thing that keeps it honest.

We have, then, the crowd: existence spread in degrees across the dimensions, the tiers ascending from stone to god to the nameless ground that is no tier at all, the three orientations and the one distinction — through versus over — that separates a discipline from a cage, the two faces of the light-bringer at the mythic center, the two frames that let the contested be held without credulity or cowardice, the economy of attention that feeds the whole, and the honest dark at the edges that no census reaches. It is a rich and dangerous population to be a stream among, and it presses the question the next part exists to answer. For if there are orientations — if some strategies run through other streams and some roll over them, if there is a light-bringer and an adversary and a real difference between them — then the clean structural fact of coherence has opened, at last, onto *value*: onto good and evil, and whether they are anything more than the names two keyholes call each other across the dark, and whether, in a cosmos with no summit to hand a law down from, anything at all can be said to bind. That is the summit of the book, and we climb it now.
