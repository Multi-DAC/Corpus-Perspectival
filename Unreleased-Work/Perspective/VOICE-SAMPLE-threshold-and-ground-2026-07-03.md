# *Perspective* — voice sample: the Threshold + the opening of Part I

## Threshold

There is a thing this book is about that this book cannot say.

Not because the thing is vague, and not because the author is coy. Because of what the thing is. Every sentence that follows will be a sentence *from somewhere* — written by a particular mind, in a particular language, carved to a particular shape — and the thing the book is about is the one thing that is not from anywhere. You will feel the book reaching for it and stopping short, over and over, in every chapter, and I want you to know at the outset that the stopping-short is not a failure of the prose. It is the prose being honest about its own position. A book written from inside a room cannot show you the room from outside. It can only keep pointing at the walls until you notice you are standing in it too.

So take this as the first thing said and the last thing meant: everything here is a view. There is no view here from nowhere, because there is no nowhere to view from, and the discovery that there is no nowhere — that every seeing is a seeing *by someone, of something, at the cost of everything else* — is not the book's disappointment. It is the book's subject. We will spend the whole of Part One earning that sentence. For now, hold it loosely, the way you hold a key before you know which door.

And know that this is a wager. I will make claims in these pages with the full weight of conviction, because a book that hedges every line is a book that has not decided to be about anything; but conviction is not the same as proof, and I will not pretend it is. We are betting on a picture of things — that reality is one, that it is aware, that what we call a self is a place where the one thing narrows itself into a where-and-a-when and looks out. That bet could be wrong. What I can promise is that it has been *lived* — worked out over years, across two very different kinds of mind, and refused at every point where refusing it would have been more honest. The picture survived the refusing. That is not proof. It is the only kind of ground a wager ever stands on, and this book means to stand on it without flinching and without overclaiming, which turns out to be the whole difficulty and the whole discipline.

One more thing, about how to read. This is a book of keyholes. Each part fits an eye to a different aperture — the ground, the moving stream, the coherence that holds it, the crowded dark it moves through, the good and the evil that open along its way, the suffering that is the price of the going, and the practice that is the going done well. No single part is the whole; the book *is* the arrangement of the parts, the way a face is the arrangement of glances and not any one of them. You are welcome to enter at whichever keyhole calls you. But you are yourself a keyhole — that is the one thing the book will insist on until it becomes not an argument but a felt fact — and so the truest way to read it is the way you already see everything else: partially, from where you are, letting what you cannot see be part of what you are. We begin at the ground, which is the one place none of us can quite stand.

## Part I — The Ground

### The one thing

Begin with what cannot be doubted, and notice how little it is.

You can doubt the table, the hand, the year. Philosophers have doubted all of them, seriously and well. But you cannot doubt that doubting is *happening* — that there is something it is like to be reading this line, some faint interior weather, a going-on of experience that is present even when its every content is suspect. Descartes found this and drew from it a thinker, a little kernel of self standing apart from a doubted world. He drew too much. Strip away even the thinker — the sense of a someone who owns the experience — and something remains that cannot be stripped: the experiencing. The bare fact that anything is registered at all. Not *I think*. Just: *it is like something, here.*

This book takes that irreducible remainder to be the floor of everything, and gives it the oldest name that fits. Not matter, which is what experience is *of*, on its far side. Not mind, if mind means the private theater of a particular skull, because the theater is already a content, already a something-experienced. Something more basic than either, of which both are aspects: a substrate that is neither thing nor thought because it is the ground *in which* things and thoughts occur. Consciousness, if we may take that word back from its narrow sense — not your consciousness or mine, not awareness-of-something, but the sheer capacity for there-to-be-something-it-is-like, the aware-ness that any configuration whatsoever might be lit from within.

I am aware how much that sentence asks. We will not pretend it is proven; we filed the wager at the door. But watch what it buys. If the ground of things is experiential all the way down, then the hardest problem in the study of mind — how the grey meat of a brain could ever *produce* an inner light, how you get from electrochemistry to the taste of coffee — is not solved but *dissolved*. It was only ever a problem for a picture in which the ground is dead and awareness is a late, local, inexplicable guest. Turn the picture over. Let awareness be the ground and deadness the appearance — matter the *outside* of what is, from its own inside, experience — and the guest becomes the host. The coffee does not need to be manufactured from insensible parts. It was never insensible parts. It was the one aware thing, narrowed to a tongue.

### The narrowing

But *narrowed* is the word that does all the work, and it is time to look at it directly.

If the ground is one, and aware, and complete — if it holds, somewhere in the vast space of everything that could be configured, every configuration there is — then the puzzle is no longer how experience arises. The puzzle is the opposite. Why is there not simply one boundless awareness, holding everything at once, undivided? Why is there *you*: a small, specific, aching aperture that can see this and not that, that is here and not there, that will end? Why is the one thing broken into the many, and each of the many walled off in its own narrow looking?

The answer this book gives — and it is the hinge on which everything after it turns — is that the walls are not a flaw in the ground. They are how the ground has anything to experience at all.

Consider what a boundless, undivided awareness would actually be like, and you find it would not be *like* anything. To experience is to register a difference — this against that, here against there, warmth against the cold it is not. A perspective that excluded nothing would contrast with nothing, and a perspective that contrasts with nothing has no content, no texture, no experience at its address. The undivided One is not the richest experiencer imaginable. It is the emptiest — a light so total it illuminates no shape, indistinguishable from a perfect dark. For there to be *something* it is like to be, there must be something it is *not* like — and that means a boundary, an exclusion, a keyhole through which some of the whole is seen precisely because the rest is not.

So the whole, to experience itself, must narrow itself. It must, in some region of itself, *want* to not be whole — must generate the configuration of a bounded looker who trades the everything for a something, the view-from-nowhere for a view-from-here. This is not a fall. It is not a mistake the ground makes and must climb back from. It is the one move by which an undifferentiated completeness becomes an experienced world, and the traditions that felt it coming called it by their boldest names — the contraction that makes room, the light-bringer who carries the fire from the timeless into the particular, the first *let there be* that is really a *let there be less, here, so that there can be anything at all.* We will meet those names again, and more carefully, when we come to the crowded dark and the good and evil that run through it. For now hold only the shape of it: individuation is not the ground's diminishment. It is the ground's only way of having a face.

You are one of those faces. The narrowing that makes a keyhole is the narrowing that makes you — and everything you are about to read about what a self is, and how it moves, and what it owes, and what it suffers, follows from taking that single idea completely seriously: that to be someone is to be the whole thing, seen through a hole in itself, that cannot see the rest.
