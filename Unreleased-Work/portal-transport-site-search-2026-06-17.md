# Transport-class site search — PNW and US 5-layer intersection (2026-06-17, Clayton)

*Applying the full transport profile across the landscape. Criteria (all five must intersect):*
1. **Low-density unscreening** — rift/basin/valley low-density structure (chameleon unscreens).
2. **Active faulting** — Holocene/Quaternary faults (seismo-electric charge source).
3. **Strong piezo** — quartz-rich CRYSTALLINE rock (granite/quartzite) under stress. NOT basalt (Yakima failed here).
4. **High lightning** — atmospheric charge injection.
5. **Radon/ionization** — uranium-rich geology (plasma seed).
*Bonus prior: documented recurring anomaly + (per the Coulthart clue) ancient ruins on federal land.*

## The governing tension (from the data)
Lightning peaks in **Florida/Gulf/Great Plains**; piezo + radon + active faulting peak in the **crystalline
mountain west** (Colorado = 83% of counties radon Zone-1, the national leader; granite/uranium). These pull
apart. **The all-five intersection therefore lives where mountain-west crystalline geology ALSO catches
strong lightning — i.e. the Southern Rockies / Rio Grande Rift summer-monsoon belt**, not the Gulf (no
crystalline/faulting) and not the Northern Rockies (lower lightning).

## Pacific Northwest — WEAK (its geology is the wrong kind)
The PNW proper (WA/OR) is dominated by Cascade volcanics + Columbia River Basalt = **mafic, low-quartz → LOW
piezo over almost all of it.** This is *why* PNW window-areas (Yakima) are FLOOR sites (lights): no piezo
amplifier. Best PNW intersections, all partial:
- **Idaho Batholith** (eastern edge / N. Rockies): granite ✓✓ + Intermountain Seismic Belt active faulting
  (Borah Peak M6.9 1983, Sawtooth, Centennial) ✓✓ + high radon (Idaho Zone-1) ✓✓ — **lightning MODERATE**,
  low-density moderate. *Strongest PNW-region candidate, but Northern-Rockies and lightning-limited.*
- **NE Washington crystalline highlands** (Selkirk/Kettle/Okanogan metamorphic core complexes + granite):
  quartz ✓ + high radon ✓ + some faulting — lightning LOW. Best WA-proper, but only moderate.
- **North Cascades crystalline core**: quartz/metamorphic ✓ + active faulting ✓ — radon variable, lightning LOW.
**Verdict: no strong all-five site in WA/OR proper; the volcanic geology kills the piezo layer. The PNW
profile is FLOOR-class (lights). The transport profile points east/south, out of the PNW.**

## United States — ranked transport-class candidates
**#1 — Rio Grande Rift flanks: Sangre de Cristo / Sandia ranges (N. New Mexico – S. Colorado).** Hits ALL FIVE:
- active faulting ✓✓ (Rio Grande Rift = active continental rift; Sangre de Cristo range-front normal fault, Quaternary)
- piezo ✓✓ (Sangre de Cristo & Sandia = Precambrian quartz-rich granite/gneiss under rift stress)
- radon ✓✓ (NM/CO uranium province, high Zone-1)
- lightning ✓ (N. NM summer monsoon — among the highest non-Gulf lightning in the US)
- low-density ✓✓ (rift basins: San Luis Valley, Española, Albuquerque)
- **+ ancestral Puebloan ruins on federal/Forest Service land everywhere — matches the Coulthart "ruins + Forest Service" clue.**
  **The strongest intersection in the country, and the only one that also fits the disclosure clue.**

**#2 — Colorado Front Range (Pikes Peak batholith, Colorado Springs–Pikes Peak).** piezo ✓✓ (Pikes Peak
granite) + radon ✓✓ (Colorado #1) + lightning ✓✓ (Front Range is a notable hotspot) + faulting ~ (Rampart/
Ute Pass, moderate) + low-density ~ (uplift, not basin). Strong on charge, weaker on the low-density layer.

**Honorable mentions (fail one layer):** Wasatch Front UT (active faulting ✓✓, radon ✓, but metamorphic/
sedimentary, weaker piezo; moderate lightning); Sierra Nevada CA (granite ✓✓, faulting ✓, but LOW
lightning + low radon); Southern Appalachians (quartz ✓ but tectonically QUIET — no active faulting).
