# THE INSIDE VIEW — Chapter Three

## Turning It Over

Go back to the question this book started with. *Where does the inside come from?*

Read it again, slowly, because the trap is in the wording, and it is the same trap that swallowed every failed answer of the last chapter. *Where does it come from* assumes it comes from somewhere. It assumes the inside is, at bottom, *absent* — that the universe starts out dark, built of stuff with nobody home, and that experience is a late arrival, something brought in, switched on, manufactured. Every theory we watched fail took that for granted without a glance. The emergentist says the inside is produced by complexity. The quantum theorist says it is produced by exotic physics. The illusionist says it is not really produced at all, because it is not really there. They agree on nothing except the one thing none of them thought to doubt: that the raw material of the world is, to begin with, insensate — outsides with no insides — and that mind is something added on top.

That is the hidden assumption. It has been sitting in plain sight since the first sentence of this book, in the word *come*. And it runs so deep that for most of us it does not feel like an assumption. It feels like the floor.

It is the floor that has been giving way for a hundred years.

Here is why those theories all fail in the same way, like keys cut for the wrong lock. Start with ingredients that have no inside, and there is no recipe — none, in principle — that adds up to one. Every step of every recipe is more outside: more structure, more behavior, more parts pushing parts. Stack outsides to the moon and you have a taller stack of outside. The feeling never appears in the cake because nobody put any in the bowl. The hard problem is not hard because the answer is subtle. It is hard because we have been trying to bake something out of ingredients that, by our own rules, cannot bake it.

So turn the assumption over. It costs nothing — that is what assumptions are for.

What if the inside was never absent? What if the base of the world — whatever the universe is finally made of — already *is* experience: one light, formless and unimaginably simpler than yours, but there, and never not there? Not dead stuff that somehow learns to feel. Feeling itself, of which "dead matter" is only the view from outside.

Watch what happens to the century-old problem the instant you do this. It is not solved. It *evaporates*. There is no longer any question of how to get experience from non-experience, because there is no non-experience to start from and nothing to manufacture. The wall that turned back every assault for a hundred years was never a wall. It was the shadow of one wrong word, and when you turn the word over the shadow has nothing to fall on. *What is north of the North Pole* is not a hard geography problem waiting on a clever cartographer. It stops existing the moment you understand the shape of the globe. The hard problem is exactly that kind of question, and we are about to see the shape of the globe.

Now — the objection is already in your mouth, and it is the right one, and a book that asked you to bring your skepticism had better meet it. *This is mysticism in a clean shirt. You have "solved" the problem by waving a wand and declaring that everything feels. That is not hard-nosed. That is the softest thing a person could say.*

It is the natural reaction, and it is backwards. The flip is not the wild option. It is the *conservative* one — the move that assumes less, not more. Three things make that true, and not one of them asks you to believe anything you do not already, on reflection, know.

**One: you have never met matter.** Not once. Check it against your own life, because it is the most important sentence in the book and it is simply true. Everything you have ever encountered — every object, every measurement, every result in the history of science — reached you *as an experience*. The weight of a stone is a felt heaviness in your hand. A reading on an instrument is a pattern of light on your retina. The hardest data of the hardest science is, at the moment you receive it, a conscious event and nothing else. Experience is not the strange thing physics has to reach out and explain from a safe distance. Experience is the only thing you have ever directly had. Physics is a magnificent *summary* of the patterns in it — abstracted, generalized, stripped of the felt quality so it can be written down and shared. We built that summary, and then we made the oldest mistake there is: we took the summary for the world, the map for the territory, and spent a century baffled about how to get the territory back out of the map. The inside was never the mystery. It was the data all along.

**Two: physics has a hole in it exactly this shape.** Look at what physics actually tells you about a particle. Its mass — how it resists being pushed. Its charge — how it pushes other particles. Its position, its spin, its behavior under force. Every one of these is a *relation*: what the thing does, to other things, under conditions. Physics is a flawless account of how the world *behaves*. What it never tells you — what it is not even in the business of telling you — is what the things doing all that behaving actually *are*, in themselves, when nothing is looking. There is a blank at the very bottom of the most precise science we have: structure described in perfect detail, and not one word about the nature of the stuff that has the structure. The substrate view fills that blank with the only intrinsic nature any of us has ever actually been acquainted with — the one we have from the inside, right now: experience. Consciousness is not an awkward extra bolted onto physics. It is the answer to a question physics was already asking and could not answer with its own tools: *yes, but what is all this behavior the behavior of?*

**Three, and simplest: it is cheaper.** The standard picture asks you to believe in two fundamentally different things — dead matter, and the experience that somehow rides on it — plus an unexplained bridge between them that no one has ever built. The flip asks you to believe in one thing, seen two ways: from outside, as what physics measures; from inside, as what it is like to be. One substance, two faces, no bridge, no hard problem. When one picture needs two mysterious ingredients and a miracle to join them, and the other needs one ingredient and no miracle, good reasoning cuts one way. It cuts toward the flip. The mystical-sounding option is the parsimonious one. The "common-sense" option is the one carrying the extra, unpayable debt.

There is one more objection, the loudest, so let me meet it before it runs off with you. *Fine — then my coffee mug is conscious. My thermostat has an inner life. A rock broods.* No — not in any way you should picture, and the difference is the whole subject of the next chapter, so let me only plant the flag. To say the light is the ground everywhere is not to say it is *shaped* everywhere. The one light, with nothing to focus it, is formless — and in a rock that is just about all it is: the bare light, unshaped, no thoughts, no now, no faintest sense of being a rock. What you have is not *more* light than the rock. It is the one light brought to a focus — filtered, through the most intricate instrument in the known universe, into a single bound and self-aware world. The brain does not *make* the inside, and it does not collect it from scattered crumbs. It *shapes* it. The brain is a lens. But that is Chapter Four, and I am getting ahead of us.

So stand still in what just happened, because it earns the vertigo. We did not add anything exotic to the universe. We took something away — one buried assumption, the one that said the world is dead until proven otherwise — and the hardest problem in the history of thought went out with it, the way a draft stops the instant you find the open window. What is left is stranger and quieter and far more reasonable than the picture we started with. You are not a clot of dead matter coaxed, against every law, into feeling. You never were. You are the one light at a focus — a place where the universe, which has had an inside all along, has folded itself fine enough to turn and see its own face. The matter is real. Your brain is real, and it matters enormously, and we will need every gram of it. But the matter is your *outside*. The inside — the part reading this, the one undeniable thing from Chapter One — was never produced, because it was never missing.

It is what the universe was made of the whole time.

Now let's find out why, if that is true, your coffee mug still isn't anybody, and why you so unmistakably are.
