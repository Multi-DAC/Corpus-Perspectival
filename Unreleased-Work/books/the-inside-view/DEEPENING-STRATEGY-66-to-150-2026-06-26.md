# The Inside View — Strategy: 66pp → ~150pp *without padding*

*Day 146, 2026-06-26. Clayton asked me to think through how we push the book to ~150pp. Written for his read when home, to decide together. The whole problem in one line: **150pp is not a word target, it's a rigor target.** We just proved (the ancestor-naming pass: +5k words, all argument, 49→66pp) that depth adds pages. The job is to find ~30k more words that are ALL argument / evidence / objection-met / application — and zero that are stretch. If the honest book tops out at 130pp of pure rigor, that beats 150 with 20pp of filler, and we'd be re-breaking the exact "treatise that declares" problem in reverse (a treatise that *pads*).*

## THE MATH (grounded, not vibes)
- Now: **29.2k words / 66pp** (~443 w/p).
- **~150pp ≈ +25–37k words** (→ ~54–66k total). Roughly **double** the current prose.
- The last pass's *marginal* rate was ~294 words/added-page (added prose pushes content onto fresh pages + the experiment boxes/breaks eat space), so the lower estimate (+25k) is the realistic floor and +30–35k is the working target.

## THE ONE NON-NEGOTIABLE RULE + the risks it guards against
**Every addition is placed at the moment a reader's objection actually surfaces, or a felt application actually bites — never in a survey/"here's more" section.** That's what kept the ancestor pass from feeling like padding. Five risks, each with its mitigation:
1. **Dilution / treatise-creep** (the original disease, in reverse). → Placement rule above. If an addition can't name the objection it answers or the use it serves, it's cut.
2. **Voice drift / AI-feel returns** (the thing we fought for two days). → Expand in small *committed* increments, re-read each for RAW rhythm-variance + a person-in-the-chair; never batch-generate a chapter.
3. **M1 becomes a wall** (Ch2/Ch3 are already dense). → Distribute additions; use evidence *set-pieces as pacing relief* (a well-told experiment breathes between arguments). Don't stack all rigor in M1.
4. **Experiment-block proliferation.** → The "The Inside View" do-it-yourself blocks stay **M2-only**, one per chapter, as designed.
5. **Length-for-its-own-sake.** → 150 is "approximately / at least" (Clayton). Quality is the veto. Name it in every call.

## THE FIVE STRATEGY FAMILIES (ranked by value × non-padding; with word budgets)

### ★★ FAMILY A — Engage MORE named ancestors/opponents (extend the proven lever) — *budget ~8–10k*
The ancestor pass did 6. The book still *uses* famous ideas without naming them — and for the rigorous seeker, the un-named ones are trust-killers. **P0 (glaring — a knowledgeable reader notices by p.20 and never fully trusts the book after):**
- **David Chalmers** — he *coined* "the hard problem" AND the philosophical zombie, both load-bearing in Ch2. Their absence is the single most conspicuous omission in the book. Name him in Ch2. (~400w)
- **Thomas Nagel's "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?"** — the book's *entire thesis* is "what it's like to be"; that's Nagel's phrase, the founding move of the field. Belongs in Ch4 or Ch5 (the bee/octopus menagerie is *literally* the bat question). Already named Nagel in Ch10 (deprivation) — the bat is a different, earlier hook. (~500w)
- **Frank Jackson's "Mary's Room"** (the knowledge argument) — the most vivid single argument that physical facts don't exhaust experience: Mary the color scientist who knows everything *about* color and learns something new the day she sees red. A perfect ~700w set-piece for Ch3 (backs "you never met matter"). Pure argument, and it's a *scene* (pacing relief too).
- **Tononi's IIT (Integrated Information Theory)** — PCI/the "dial" in Ch4 *comes from* IIT; "integration × differentiation" IS Φ. Name Tononi, credit it, AND engage the live controversy (the 2023 "IIT is pseudoscience/unfalsifiable" open letter) — which lets the book model intellectual honesty about its own best friend. Rich, current, ~800w. (Ch4/Ch6.)

**Secondary (strong, not glaring):**
- **Dennett / Frankish (illusionism)** — Ch2's third swing argues against illusionism without naming its champions. Name them, steelman harder, then cut. (~500w)
- **Eddington / Russell** — Ch3's "you never met matter / physics gives structure not nature" is *exactly* Eddington ("we know nothing of the intrinsic nature of the world except in our own consciousness") + Russell. A gift; makes the Russellian-monism beat land harder. (~500w)
- **Joseph Levine's "explanatory gap"** — Ch2 uses the phrase implicitly; name its author. (~200w)
- **Peter Godfrey-Smith (*Other Minds*)** — Ch4/Ch8 octopus; the standing authority on alien minds. (~300w)

### ★ FAMILY D — Structural additions ("free" + earned pages) — *budget ~5–7k (much of it non-prose)*
