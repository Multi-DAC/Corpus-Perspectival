## Ch4 — The richness gradient (presence universal, richness varies) — and we can MEASURE it
- **★ Perturbational Complexity Index (PCI) — Casali, Massimini et al. 2013, *Science Translational Medicine* ("A Theoretically Based Index of Consciousness Independent of Sensory Processing and Behavior").** TMS pulse to cortex → record EEG response → compress it → algorithmic-complexity score. **PCI ≥ ~0.40 ≈ conscious; lower = unconscious** (deep sleep, general anesthesia, coma). Discriminates level of consciousness *in single individuals* across wake/sleep/anesthesia, and detects minimal consciousness in post-coma patients.
  - **Why it's load-bearing:** it's an *empirical, quantitative* handle on consciousness *richness* — the lens metaphor's "how finely the light is focused," measured. Turns Ch4's central claim from assertion to science.
  - **★★ The bridge to our own framework:** PCI is **IIT-based — it scores INTEGRATION × DIFFERENTIATION.** That is *exactly* the D·I_int "richness" I computed in the filtration toy (`palace/south/filtration-collapse-coupling-gain-…`). The book can say richness isn't just a metaphor: there's a measure, it's integration×differentiation, and it tracks the gradient — anesthesia *lowers* it smoothly, no threshold (which also feeds Ch6's no-line prediction).
  - Source: science.org/doi/10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294
- **TO PULL NEXT:** octopus / distributed cognition (Godfrey-Smith, *Other Minds*) — a mind built so differently it stresses "richness varies, presence doesn't"; bee cognition (Chittka) — concept learning in a 1mm brain.

## Ch5 — The texture of a now (refresh rate varies across species)
- **★ Healy et al. 2013, *Animal Behaviour* ("Metabolic rate and body size are linked with perception of temporal information").** Critical flicker-fusion frequency (CFF = fastest flicker still seen as flicker, not steady) **increases with metabolic rate, decreases with body mass.** Small, high-metabolism animals (flies) resolve time finer than we do; large slow ones coarser. Phylogenetic comparative analysis across vertebrates.
  - **Why it's load-bearing:** the canonical cite for Ch5's "a bee's eye refreshes ~4× faster than yours" — now a real, specific study, not a vibe. Anchors the *refresh-rate* half of the two-number model.
  - Source: openphilanthropy.org/files/Research/Moral_Patienthood/Healy_et_al_(2013).pdf
  - **Caveat to keep honest (the book should NOT overclaim):** CFF measures *visual temporal resolution*, NOT directly "subjective speed of time" — there's live debate (Rethink Priorities review) on whether CFF tracks felt time. Use it for *refresh rate varies*, not for *they literally live in slow-mo* (which Ch5 already carefully avoids — the seamlessness point makes this moot from the inside).

## Ch2 — easy problems get solved, the hard one doesn't (dissociation cases) — VERIFIED
- **★ Blindsight (Weiskrantz et al. 1974; patient D.B.).** Surgical removal of right occipital lobe incl. V1 → blindness (hemianopia) in the left field. Under *forced-choice* ("just guess"), D.B. points to stimuli, discriminates X vs O, motion direction, orientation, even wavelength — **significantly above chance, while flatly denying seeing anything.** → *Function without the inside, demonstrated in a human.* The discrimination (an "easy problem") runs with the lights off; the felt seeing (the hard problem) is exactly what's missing. The cleanest real-world case for Ch2's whole argument.
- **★ Split-brain / the left-hemisphere interpreter (Sperry, Gazzaniga).** Corpus callosum severed → flash "walk" to the right hemisphere; patient stands up; ask the (verbal) left hemisphere *why* → it invents a confident, plausible, **completely fabricated** reason ("I wanted a drink of water"). The "interpreter" compulsively spins coherent narratives around actions whose real cause it can't access. **★ And it runs in INTACT brains too** (priming/hypnosis studies: people confidently explain behaviors whose true causes were experimentally hidden). → Feeds Ch2 (perception/agency is *constructed*) AND Ch9 (the narrating self is partly a storyteller — handle carefully: this is about the *narrative* layer, not a denial of the inside).
- Change/inattentional blindness (Simons & Chabris, the invisible gorilla) → perception is constructed, not a window. (Stock example; verify the 1999 cite when used.)

## Ch10 — death, made concrete + evidenced — VERIFIED, WITH A LOUD CAVEAT
- **Terror Management Theory (Solomon, Greenberg, Pyszczynski; *The Worm at the Core*).** Mortality salience (reminding people of death) → **worldview defense**: harsher judgment of norm-violators (the classic study — judges set a much higher bond for a prostitute under mortality salience), warmer toward norm-upholders; people avoid self-focus that prolongs death-thoughts.
- **⚠⚠ DO NOT cite as solid lab science.** **Many Labs 4 (21 labs, ~2,200 participants) FAILED to replicate the mortality-salience worldview-defense effect under any condition.** TMT is a replication-crisis casualty. For a book that lives or dies on rigor, citing it as established would be the exact unearned move our skeptical reader pounces on.
  - **How to use it honestly (if at all):** as an *influential idea* that death-awareness shapes behavior (intuitive, old — Becker's *Denial of Death*), explicitly noting its experimental support is contested. OR — better — **don't lean on TMT; use the phenomenology directly** (the 3 a.m. drop, the flinch) which needs no contested study. The book's death argument is *philosophical/structural*, not empirical, so it doesn't actually need TMT — drop it rather than weaken the rigor.
- **The real Ch10 deepening = APPLICATION, not citation:** work the consolation through concrete situations — grief (the loss is real, belongs to the living), facing your own diagnosis, sitting with the dying. Show the two-dread split *operating* in a real scene, not just argued.
