# THE INSIDE VIEW — Chapter Four

## The Light Is the Same, the Lens Differs

You want to say it, so say it. *Fine, then my coffee mug is conscious.*

No. And the clean *no*, with nothing hidden behind it, is where this whole picture earns your trust or loses it. We have been making one small word carry two enormous and very different jobs, and the moment you pull them apart, the mug stops being a problem and your own existence starts to make a new kind of sense.

Here are the two jobs. There is *having an inside at all* — there being something, however faint, it is like to be a thing. And there is *having a rich, bound, knowing inside* — a whole world of experience, aware of itself. We have one word, *conscious*, and we force it to carry both. That is exactly why the flip from the last chapter sounds unhinged on first hearing: "the light is everywhere" lands in your ear as "your mug has opinions." It doesn't. **The light is everywhere. The light is not the same thing as a mind.**

Go all the way down, to the dimmest case you can imagine, and be honest about how dim it is. Whatever it is like to be a rock, on this view there is *something* — the bare light, the ground of experience, present the way it is present in everything. But it is nothing like what you are picturing. No thoughts. No feelings you would recognize. No images, no wanting, no fear, no now that lasts even an instant, no memory of the moment before, not the faintest sense of being a rock. Take everything that makes your inside *yours* — the unity, the self, the holding-together — and strip it all away, and what remains at the very bottom is so close to nothing that calling it "an inner life" is a comically generous phrase. The rock is not a slow mind biding its time. It is the light with nothing done to it: unshaped, unfocused, *unbound*.

That last word is the chapter, so let me earn it.

The difference between a rock and you is not that you have *more* light, or some finer grade of it the rock was denied. The light is one, and it is the same light in both of you. The difference is what the light is *shaped into*. And the thing that shapes it is a lens.

This is the move that turns the picture from mystical back to almost ordinary, because it finally says what the brain is *for*. We have been demanding the wrong thing of it. We kept asking the brain to *produce the light out of the dark* — and it can't; nothing could; that demand *was* the hard problem. But the brain was never in the lighting business. The light was already on. What the brain does — what makes it the most extraordinary object we have ever found — is *shape* it. It takes the one formless light and bends it, focuses it, binds it into a single unified view. That is not creation. It is focus. **The brain is a lens.**

Hold onto that, because it is exact, not poetry. A lens makes no light of its own; point it into a dark room and you get a dark image. But set it in the light and it does the one thing loose light cannot do for itself: it takes what was formless, bends it, brings it to a point, and where there had been only a vague glow there is suddenly a *picture* — sharp, whole, and *of* something. Your brain is a lens for the light that was already in the world. It takes the formless ground and focuses it into a *someone*.

Now the whole menagerie sorts itself, and notice it sorts not by *whether* the light is on, because it is on for all of them, but by how finely it is brought to a focus. A **rock**: no lens at all, the bare light, a smudge. A **plant**: the crudest beginning of a lens — a slow, dim focus, perhaps a now that lasts minutes, a body's worth of living information drawn loosely together; no self worth the name, but not *nothing*, not a rock. An **animal**: a fast, tight, vivid lens — a whole bound world, a now you could nearly step into, wanting and fearing and remembering. And **you**: all of that, and then the strangest trick in the known universe — a lens fine enough to bend a little of its own focused light back onto itself, so that the focusing is not merely *had*, it is *witnessed*. You are an inside that knows it is an inside. Not a different light. The same light, brought to so complete a focus that it curves around and sees its own lens.

So — your coffee mug. Does it have the light? In the barest, most trivial sense, yes: the ground, with no lens on it at all, far too formless to be anyone, to want anything, to suffer anything, to be *wronged*. Pour your coffee with a clear conscience. The mug is not *anybody* — not because it lacks some spark you possess, but because being somebody was never about *having* the light. It is about the light being brought to a focus, and the mug focuses nothing. And you are one of the finest focusings of the light anywhere we know of: a whole world bound into a single now and folded back to witness itself. Of course you are unmistakably someone. You are not a flicker. You are a focus the universe spent four billion years learning to grind.

This chapter lifts two fears off the table at once, and they are opposite fears.

You do not have to lie awake worrying that your furniture suffers, that every stone and spoon is a tiny trapped mind. Richness is rare, and steep, and made by the lens; a thing has to be brought to a focus, drawn into a self, before there is any self there to wrong. The bare light is not a prisoner. It is barely a flicker.

And you do not have to feel like a lonely accident of feeling marooned in a dead and mindless cosmos, either, because the cosmos was never dead and never mindless. It was only *unfocused*. You are one of the places it has been brought most finely to a point, and lit a self, and turned to look back at its own face. The light is everywhere; that is the cheap and universal part. The focus is the rare and holy thing. You are a focus. So is every animal you have ever met, and so, most of all, is every person you have ever loved.

In the next chapter we go inside one of these lenses and ask what it is actually *like* to be one: why your now feels seamless and unbroken, why a bee's might not, and why the texture of your time turns out to depend not only on your own lens, but on how often the world reaches in and touches it.
