# THE INSIDE VIEW — Chapter Seven

## The Only Moment There Is

*Movement Two — what changes.*

Try something simple, right now: experience a moment other than this one.

You can't. Not because you aren't trying hard enough, but because there is nowhere else the experiencing can happen. You can remember this morning, and the remembering happens *now*. You can picture tomorrow, and the picturing happens *now*. Every second of your life, without one exception, has been undergone at a single point: the present, where the light comes to its focus in you.

It is tempting to take that and run somewhere it doesn't go — to decide that since you only ever experience the present, the past and the future must be unreal, mental smoke to be cleared away. But that isn't what follows, and it isn't what this book has been building. The light is one, and it is everything: not only this moment but every moment, every region of it real and lit and whole, as actual as this one. The morning you call gone is not gone. The tomorrow you call coming is not waiting to be born. They are *there* — the way the far reaches of a vast country are there while you stand in one field of it at night.

What is special about *now* is not that it is the only real place. It is the only place your lens is currently focused.

That is close to true, so picture it that way. You are a lens, and the one light pours through you. But a lens can only bring one stretch of the light to a focus at a time, and the focusing — the seeing, the being — happens *there*, at the bright point, always. That bright point is the present. It is not a thin line trapped between two darknesses you have lost. It is the one place anything is ever in focus, and it goes with you wherever you turn.

And here is the part that changes everything: *you can aim it.* Not perfectly, not anywhere at once, but you steer. Where you put your attention is, to a real degree, what comes into focus — what gets lit, what you actually undergo. The present is not a cell you are locked in. It is the one place from which you can point.

This recasts the long ache that runs under a life — not the body's sharp, honest pain, but the quiet suffering with no wound to point to. So much of it is the lens held still, for hours, on a single dark region: a hurt that already happened, a fear that hasn't and may not. Those regions are real — I will not insult your griefs and dreads by calling them illusions; they are as real as any other part of the light. But you have been holding the focus there, in the dark, by a choice you did not know you were making, when the aim was yours to turn the whole time.

So the freedom is not *the past and future aren't real, so let them go*. That is a thinner, colder comfort, and it isn't even true. The freedom is *you are the one aiming*. You can let the focus rest where it rests — sometimes a region asks to be sat with, and sitting with it is its own kind of steering — but you are never only its prisoner. The bright point moves when you move it.

And this makes room for the one thing a pure, time-dissolving stillness always loses: *hope*. If the future were unreal, hope would be a mistake, a leaning toward smoke. But the future is a real stretch of the light, and you are turning toward it. Hope is not a flight out of the present. It is the present aimed forward — the lens swinging, gently, toward a region you mean to reach, the felt pull of a navigation that has somewhere to go. You do not have to choose between being here and hoping. Being here, and turning toward it, *is* how the hoping is done.

There is a consolation under all of this, and the rigor pays for it. You cannot fall out of the present, and you have never missed your life. The worry that it is all rushing past while you are distracted is just the focus landing, for a moment, on a region labeled *regret* — real, but not a place you are condemned to stand. Whatever happens in your stream, you are *by definition* at its focus. There is no version of you stranded back in a dark region of the past, none waiting unlit in the future. There is only the one of you, here, where the light comes to its point — exactly where you have always been, and exactly where the aiming is done from.

So that is the first thing that changes when you stop treating the inside view as a problem and start treating it as the ground you stand on: the present stops being a cage and becomes a vantage. Not a place you are stuck — the one place you can aim from, onto a world that is entirely real and entirely yours to move through.

There is a second thing, and it follows from standing — really standing — in the lit present, doing your own aiming. Because when you finally look up from the one region you had been staring at, and out across the rest of the light, you notice something the dark had been hiding.

The other lenses. The other places the one light is coming to a focus and looking back.

You were never the only one.
