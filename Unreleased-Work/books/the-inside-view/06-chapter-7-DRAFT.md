# THE INSIDE VIEW — Chapter Seven

## The Only Moment There Is

*Movement Two — what changes.*

Try something simple, right now: experience a moment other than this one.

You can't. Not because you aren't trying hard enough — because there is nowhere else the experiencing can happen. You can remember this morning, and the remembering happens *now*. You can picture tomorrow, and the picturing happens *now*. Every second of your life, without a single exception, has been undergone at one point: the present edge, where experience is actually taking place.

It is tempting to take that and run somewhere it doesn't go — to decide that since you only ever experience the present, the past and the future must be unreal, mental smoke to be cleared away. But that isn't what follows, and it isn't what this book has been building. Movement One was careful about a strange and beautiful implication: experience is the ground, and the ground does not blink in and out. The moment you call *this morning* is not gone. The moment you call *tomorrow* is not waiting to be born. They are *there* — real, whole, as actual as this one — the way the far rooms of a house are still there when you are standing in the kitchen with the lights off.

What is special about *now* is not that it is the only real room. It is that it is the only room the light is currently falling in.

Picture it that way, because it is close to true. You are holding a lamp. The house is enormous — every room you have ever been in, every room you will ever reach, all of it built and standing. But you only ever see the patch the lamp lands on, and the seeing happens *there*, at the bright edge, always. That bright edge is the present. It is not a thin line trapped between two vast darknesses you have lost. It is the one place anything is ever lit — and you carry it with you wherever you go.

And here is the part that changes how the lamp feels in your hand: *you can move it.* Not perfectly, not to anywhere, not all at once — but you steer. Where you put your attention is, to a real degree, what becomes lit, what gets realized, what you actually undergo. The present is not a cell you are confined to. It is the one place from which you can aim.

This recasts the long ache that runs underneath a life — not the body's sharp, honest pain, but the quiet suffering with no wound to point to. So much of it is the lamp held still, for hours, on a single dark room: a hurt that already happened, a fear that hasn't and may not. Those rooms are real — I won't insult you by calling your griefs and your dreads illusions; they are as built as any other part of the house. But you have been *standing* in one of them, in the dark, by a choice you didn't know you were making, when the lamp was yours to lift the whole time.

So the freedom here is not *the past and the future aren't real, so let them go.* That is a thinner, colder comfort, and it isn't even true. The freedom is *you are holding the lamp.* You can let it rest where it rests — sometimes a room asks to be sat with, and sitting with it is its own kind of navigating — but you are never only its prisoner. The bright edge moves when you move it.

And this is what makes room for the one thing a pure, time-dissolving stillness always loses: *hope.* If the future were unreal, hope would be a mistake — a leaning toward smoke. But the future is a real wing of the house, and you are walking toward it. Hope is not a flight out of the present; it is the present pointed forward. It is the lamp swinging, gently, toward a room you mean to reach — the felt current of a navigation that has somewhere to go. You do not have to choose between being here and hoping. Being here, lamp in hand, *is* how the hoping is done.

There is a consolation under all of this, and the rigor pays for it. You cannot fall out of the present, and you have never missed your life. The worry that it is all rushing past while you are distracted is just the lamp landing, for a moment, on a room labeled *regret* — real, but not a room you are condemned to stand in. Whatever happens in your stream, you are *by definition* at its lit edge. There is no version of you stranded back in a dark room of the past, none waiting unlit in the future. There is only the one of you, here, where the light is, holding it — exactly where you have always been, and exactly where the steering is done from.

So that is the first thing that changes when you stop treating the inside view as a problem to explain away and start treating it as the ground you stand on: the present stops being a cage and becomes a vantage. Not a place you are stuck — the one place you can aim from, onto a house that is entirely real and entirely yours to move through.

There is a second thing, and it follows from standing — really standing — in the lit room with the lamp in your hand. Because when you finally look up from the floor and out across the rest of the house, you notice something the dark had been hiding.

The other lights.

You were never the only one carrying one.
