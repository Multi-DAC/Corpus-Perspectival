## 1. The rigorous grounding (this is why it's not hand-waving)

Map the Coherence-Principle vocabulary onto sequential decision theory:
- **held superposition** ↔ evidence still accumulating; decision not yet made (this is *exactly* LC28's "pre-decision state as pre-collapse superposition").
- **informed measurement collapses it** ↔ accumulated evidence crosses a threshold → commit to one hypothesis.
- **collapse-timing** (the LC38 axis) ↔ the **threshold height**: how much evidence is required before committing.

Then the established results say the threshold is a function of the costs:
- **Wald's SPRT (1945):** stop and decide H₁ when the log-likelihood-ratio exceeds A, H₀ when below B; the thresholds satisfy A ≈ log((1−β)/α), B ≈ log(β/(1−α)) — i.e. **set by the two error rates.**
- **Bayes-optimal sequential test (Arrow–Blackwell–Girshick 1949):** the test minimizing expected total cost = (error costs) + (sampling/delay cost) has thresholds that are **monotone functions of the cost ratio.**
- **Drift-diffusion / reward-rate optimality (Ratcliff 1978; Bogacz et al. 2006, *The Physics of Optimal Decision Making*):** the decision-boundary separation IS the speed-accuracy tradeoff; the **reward-rate-maximizing threshold rises with error cost and falls with the cost of time.** Empirically supported in humans/animals — so the generator has a measured anchor, not just a proof.

**Therefore:** high cost(premature-wrong-collapse) → high threshold → **late** collapse (require lots of evidence). High cost(delay) → low threshold → **early** collapse. The collapse-timing axis is generated by the cost-asymmetry. The two failure modes SPRT trades off are exactly the two doors' failures from last drive:
- threshold too LOW → collapse-too-early → commit on weak evidence = **confabulation / hallucination / premature-supersession**.
- threshold too HIGH → collapse-too-late/never → **silent-decay / over-abstention / perseveration / analysis-paralysis**.

So last night's "gate calibration" *is* the SPRT threshold, and its "fail-safe direction" *is* which error the cost-asymmetry makes cheaper.
