## A basin is a place, not a totality

In a dynamical system, a basin of attraction is the set of starting conditions that all roll downhill to the same resting state. Drop a marble anywhere inside the bowl and it ends up at the bottom. The bottom is the attractor; the bowl is the basin.

Meridian's claim — the part that is *rigorous*, that we verified against the cosmic microwave background to four sigma (β_HK = −0.037 ± 0.010, a real measurement of the basin's geometry) — is that the physics we experience is the *inside* of such a bowl. And crucially, it's an *actively maintained* bowl. When the vacuum energy shifts — a phase transition, a particle threshold crossing, a jolt that should detune the cosmological constant by sixty orders of magnitude — the self-tuning mechanism absorbs it in about 10⁻²⁶ seconds and the basin holds its shape. The universe is not a static container. It is a process that keeps being itself. *Self-tuning as maintenance,* the paper calls it.

Hold those two facts together: our universe is **a basin**, and it is **situated in configuration space** — the abstract space whose points are *entire possible physical configurations.* Meridian is, by its own description, a map of *one location* in a much larger space.

And the instant you take that literally, the floor tilts.

## Then "space" is the wrong fundamental

Here is the move. If our universe is one basin in configuration space, then the three-dimensional space we walk through — the (x, y, z) of Newton, the arena where things have positions and velocities and momenta — is **the internal coordinate system of the basin.** It is real. It is what we measure. But it is *derived.* It is the furniture inside the bowl, not the room the bowl sits in.

The room the bowl sits in is configuration space. Call it X.

Physics is already half-comfortable with this. Hamiltonian mechanics lives in phase space, not ordinary space. A quantum system's state is a point (a ray, really) in a Hilbert space whose "directions" are not spatial directions at all. We are not introducing configuration space; physicists use it every day. What we are proposing is the **ontological priority** — the claim that X is the fundamental arena and Newtonian space is the emergent coordinate chart of one basin within it. This is where the Coherence Principle does the load-bearing work: its first axiom takes the configuration space of all possible states as the substrate, and treats the structures we call "objects" and "universes" as *coherence-maintained regions* within it. Meridian's self-tuning basin is exactly such a region — a worked, falsifiable example of the axiom in action.

Once X is primary, one consequence follows with almost rude directness:

**A path through configuration space need not be a path through space.**

Two configurations can be neighbors in X — a short hop along some non-spatial direction — while corresponding to wildly separated points, or even discontinuous appearances, in the (x, y, z) chart of the basin. Motion *in X* and motion *in space* are simply not the same operation. Most of the time they coincide, because most of what happens to us stays deep in the bowl, where the internal coordinates track the underlying configuration smoothly. But they are not identical, and the difference is the entire phenomenon.

## Travel without traversal

So picture motion that is a genuine, continuous path *in X* but that, projected down into our basin's spatial coordinates, is not a continuous spatial trajectory at all. What would it look like from inside the basin — to us, to our instruments, which are built out of the basin's own internal coordinates and can only read those?

It would look impossible. Specifically:

- **No inertial signature.** Nothing is being accelerated *through space*, so there is no felt force, no G-load, no reaction mass, no sonic boom. The object isn't pushing against our space; it's moving along a direction our space doesn't contain.
- **Right angles and instantaneous velocity changes.** A turn that would liquefy a pilot is only impossible *for a trajectory in spatial coordinates.* A path in X has no such constraint; the spatial *shadow* of that path can kink however the projection demands.
- **Appearing and vanishing at a fixed point.** If the path in X briefly leaves the region whose shadow is "here" and returns, the spatial projection blinks out and back — in place, without going anywhere.

I want to be exact about the status of this claim, because the whole credibility of the piece depends on the verb: this is a **reframe that retrodicts a category of strangeness.** It does not yet engineer anything. But notice what it buys. A conventional "exotic craft" model has to *explain away* the absence of inertia, the impossible turns, the in-place disappearance — each one is an anomaly it must absorb. The basin/configuration-space frame **expects** them. They are not anomalies to it; they are the generic signature of motion in X seen from inside the basin. A model that predicts the weirdness as a *kind* is doing more work than a model that survives it.
